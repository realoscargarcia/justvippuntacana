Group trip to Punta Cana: how to organise it without losing half a day in transfers

Published 21 December 2026 · Just VIP Punta Cana

A group trip to Punta Cana usually goes wrong in the same place: the first afternoon. Twelve people land on three different flights, someone has booked a taxi, someone else is waiting by the exit with no signal, and the suitcases do not fit in the car that turned up. By the time everyone reaches the hotel, half a day has gone.

None of this has much to do with Punta Cana itself. It comes down to a few decisions made before anyone flies: how many vehicles, who waits for whom, and who holds the list. Here they are in order, with our own fleet as the reference.

Count seats and suitcases, not just people

The first question is not how many people are coming but how many people plus how many bags. A group of six with carry-ons is a different job from a group of six with golf clubs, a pushchair and a wedding dress in a garment bag.

Our fleet gives a practical scale. The Lincoln Navigator and the Cadillac Escalade seat up to 6 passengers with room for 5 suitcases. The two Chevrolet Suburbans also seat up to 6 but take 6 suitcases, which makes them the better choice when every passenger brings a full-size case. The Mercedes-Benz Sprinter seats up to 14 with space for 14 suitcases, so a group that size travels together, luggage included, in one vehicle.

A simple way to plan:

  • Up to 6 people, light luggage: one SUV.
  • Up to 6 people, a large suitcase each: a Suburban, for the sixth case.
  • 7 to 14 people: one Sprinter, or two SUVs if the group is splitting up anyway.
  • More than 14: more than one vehicle, arranged together.

One van or several cars? One vehicle keeps everyone on the same timetable and makes the arrival simple: one meeting point, one chauffeur, one departure. Two or three SUVs give flexibility when part of the group is staying at a different hotel, or when people land hours apart. Because our rates are per vehicle, not per passenger, the comparison is easy to make: on the current rate table for Punta Cana and Cap Cana, the Sprinter is $85 one way and the least expensive SUV is $65, so one van usually costs less than two SUVs. Uvero Alto and other destinations are quoted separately; every rate is on the fleet page.

List awkward items separately: golf bags, diving gear, pushchairs, a wheelchair, instruments, boxes for an event. They decide whether a vehicle really fits.

When flights land at different times

Groups rarely arrive on one plane. Most visitors to the area land at Punta Cana International Airport (PUJ), so the real question is how to handle the gap between the first landing and the last.

Wait for everyone. If the flights land close together, the early arrivals wait and the whole group leaves in one vehicle. This works when the gap is short and nobody minds a coffee in the terminal. It works badly when the gap is three or four hours and the first arrivals have small children or a dinner booking.

Split the pickups. If the landings are far apart, book one pickup per flight, or per cluster of flights, in a vehicle sized for that part of the group. A couple on the morning flight goes in an SUV; the ten who land in the afternoon go in the Sprinter. Nobody spends the first day of the holiday in arrivals.

Whichever you choose, send the flight number for every arrival, not just the first one. We monitor arrivals at PUJ, so the chauffeur works from the real landing time, with free waiting time. When the pickups are split, a delay on one flight then stays with that flight instead of rippling into everyone else's.

One person holds the list

The groups that arrive smoothly almost always have one organiser who holds the list and talks to the transfer company. It does not have to be the person paying; it should be the person who answers WhatsApp.

The list that person needs is short:

  • The name of each traveller, grouped by flight.
  • Flight number, date and scheduled landing time for every arrival.
  • Number of suitcases and any oversized items on each flight.
  • The exact name of the hotel or villa, as it appears on the reservation, plus the address for a private villa.
  • One mobile number per flight that will be switched on at landing.
  • The return date, flight and time for each person.

The hotel name deserves its own line. Punta Cana has many resorts with similar names, several belonging to the same chain, so the chain name alone is not enough. Copy the full name from the booking confirmation. For a villa or an apartment, send the address and a map pin.

Send the whole list in one message rather than in pieces over a week. With one point of contact on each side, changes are easy to track: when someone's flight changes, the organiser updates the list and sends it again. Corporate groups can have the same arrangement for teams and guests, with one point of contact and consolidated billing.

The first evening and group outings

Once everyone has arrived, the next pinch point is the first evening. A group of ten deciding where to eat at seven o'clock, then trying to find three taxis, is how a good day ends badly. Decide in advance, and make the group move once.

To pick where to take the group on the first night, on Roavi (roavi.com) you can message a local in Punta Cana for free; each Local Friend sets their own hourly rate, is paid directly, and Roavi takes no commission.

With a plan, the transport is simple. If the restaurant is a single trip there and back, book it as a transfer each way. If the evening has several stops, or you would rather not fix a return time, hourly hire keeps the same car and chauffeur with the group for the evening; it is quoted on request. The same goes for outings during the week: private excursions to Saona, Macao or Altos de Chavón are arranged with waiting time included, so the vehicle that takes the group out is the one that brings it back. For a wedding or a celebration, send the full schedule of the day and we plan the vehicles around it.

Coming back to the airport

The return is easier, because the times are known in advance, but groups still trip on it. People often leave on different flights, and hotel check-out times rarely match flight times.

Plan the return from the same list: group the travellers by departure flight, then work back to a collection time from each hotel, leaving enough margin for check-in at PUJ. The collection time is part of the written confirmation, so everyone knows when to be in the lobby. If the whole group flies out within a short window, one vehicle can collect everyone; if not, split the return the same way you split the arrival. Booking the return with the arrival saves the organiser a second round of messages.
